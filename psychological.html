Summary

A composed, highly competent former software engineer who has built her adult life around systems she can understand and control. She is psychologically fit for extended solo flight and in several respects better suited to it than to life in the populated systems. Her principal risk is not instability but withdrawal: she has twice disappeared from service for long periods without explanation, she declines to discuss either absence, and the expedition she is about to undertake has no defined end point. No psychiatric diagnosis is made.

Watch her hands Two-finger tapping, steady at rest. Faster when a question lands too close; slows almost to stillness when she is engaged, followed by one technical question in return. RELIABLE ENGAGEMENT INDICATOR

Presentation

Arrived on time, in a Gutamaya flight suit, hair tied back. Formal Imperial bearing throughout. Speech low, measured and grammatically precise, with a dry humour usually directed at herself. Eye contact good on technical subjects, reduced on personal ones. Affect restricted but appropriate; she smiled twice, both times when describing a ship.

She tapped two fingers on the arm of the chair for most of both sessions. The rhythm was steady during discussion of her fleet and engineering, accelerated sharply when asked about the years 3308 to 3310 and 3310 to 3312, and slowed almost to stillness when the interviewer asked about the Caspian's drive tuning. When the tapping slowed she asked a technical question in return. The examiner regards this as a reliable indicator of engagement versus threat and recommends future assessors watch her hands rather than her face.

Oriented, alert, no evidence of thought disorder, perceptual disturbance or intoxication. Insight good. Judgement good in operational matters; see risk assessment for exceptions.

Background

Raised in Achenar. Describes her upbringing in a single sentence and moves on. Nine to ten years as a software engineer on Gutamaya's cockpit voice (COVAS) team. Left in 3308 at 34, by her account after realising she had spent a decade writing "the calm voice that tells a pilot their hull is failing" without ever being the pilot. This is the clearest statement of motive she offered in either session, and the examiner considers it genuine.

Career since then has been rapid and broad: exploration, Federation contract work to Ensign, Imperial Navy rank to Viscount within a month of joining, Powerplay rank 5 within a week of renewing her pledge. Two absences, 3308 to 3310 and 3310 to 3312, are undocumented. The second followed directly on a Thargoid encounter in the Pleiades in September 3310 and ended with her return to the same moon in October 3312, where she survived a systems shutdown that left a burn on her right forearm.

Single. Lives alone. No named next of kin on file. Owns thirteen ships and has sold or lost very few in four years.

Personality structure

OpennessHigh. Curious, self-taught across domains, drawn to new and unmapped things. Curiosity is structured: she researches before she goes.
ConscientiousnessVery high. Plans to module level, routes engineers in optimal order, keeps written lists and checks them. Perfectionist tendencies are present but functional; she can declare a job "as finished as it gets" and stop.
ExtraversionLow. Restored by solitude and long quiet. Socially capable and polite, not socially driven.
AgreeablenessModerate. Cooperative and fair with strangers, unwilling to be pushed. Does not seek approval and is mildly suspicious of praise.
Emotional stabilityHigh under acute stress, moderate over the long term. Recovers quickly from immediate danger; carries older material unprocessed.
Cognitive styleThinks like a programmer. Reads situations as systems with faults, inputs and versions; under uncertainty she gathers data, models it and acts on the model. An excellent long-range pilot and a poor reporter of her own emotional state, which she describes in technical terms ("the numbers moved," "it held") rather than names.
ValuesCompetence, self-reliance, privacy and loyalty to what she has chosen. Her allegiance to Li Yong-Rui is pragmatic by her own account. Her loyalty to her ships is not.

Attachment and relationships

Guarded, slow to trust, warm once trust is established. Attachment is invested far more readily in ships than in people. She keeps every ship she has loved, names them with care, and before her first flight after returning in 3312 she climbed into each of nine stored hulls and sat in it briefly. She described this as "ridiculous" and did it anyway. The examiner reads the fleet as her household: stable, always where she left it, and incapable of leaving her.

She reports no current partner and declined to name past ones. Private about her sexuality, which she disclosed as bisexual with a strong preference for women only when asked directly and only because the form required it. Occasional short-term encounters away from home, with clear terms and no follow-up, are consistent with her general pattern of keeping intimacy bounded and departure available. This is not considered maladaptive in itself. It does mean she will have no personal contact who expects to hear from her during the expedition.

She shows genuine concern for strangers in distress (repeated rescue of occupied escape pods, care for live cargo she had no duty to check on) and is uncomfortable with violence. She described bounty work as something she "didn't enjoy" and "isn't meant to."

Coping: adaptive

  • Task absorption. Engineering, route planning and sampling routines settle her quickly. She describes the exobiology cycle of jump, scan, map, launch, fly and sample as the first time in years she has "not thought about anything else for hours."
  • Humour. Dry and self-directed, used to discharge embarrassment and fear without denying either.
  • Physical routine. Keeps to a standard low-g conditioning routine wherever she is docked, to hold bone density and muscle mass against long periods in low gravity. She treats it as maintenance, the same way she treats her ships.
  • Retreat to quiet. After a near-loss of Invictus at Luyten's Star she flew the same night to an empty moon and spent time prospecting by hand. This restored her.

Coping: less adaptive

  • Deflection. Personal questions are redirected into technical detail. She does not announce the change of subject; she simply makes it.
  • Compartmentalisation. Effective in the short term. The two absences, the Thargoid encounter and the 69 ejected passengers on her record are sealed off entirely. When asked about the passengers she said, "No," and nothing else.
  • Control through modification. She is planning a heavily customised COVAS suite linked to her own biometrics, intended to suppress low-priority announcements under stress and execute emergency macros by voice, staying "as close to illegal as I can without crossing that line." The examiner notes the obvious link to her former career and does not consider the project itself a concern. The neural interface port at the base of her skull is relevant here: she again declined to disclose its firmware version.

Risk assessment for solo long-range operation

Acute incidentLow
Prolonged unreported absenceModerate

Protective factors

Exceptional technical competence. Thorough preparation, including a field maintenance unit and a route plan checked against telemetry. Calm, fast and correct decisions under acute threat. Comfort with isolation. Strong attachment to her ship, which in her case functions as a reason to come home intact. Low risk-seeking: she submits to interdictions she cannot win and leaves fights she does not need.

Strengths

Precision. Patience. Mechanical and systems insight that would serve any crew. An unforced courtesy toward strangers and an unwillingness to hurt people that has survived combat contracts. A capacity for wonder that she undersells: she remembers the colour of every organism she has sampled.

Risk factors

  • Withdrawal. Twice she has vanished for around two years. She has paid long-term rent on her hangar space and apartment "for longer than I like to think about," and stated she has no fixed return date. The examiner considers a third extended absence the single most likely adverse outcome of this expedition.
  • Isolation. No partner, no next of kin on file, no one who will notice if she stops reporting. The expedition removes her from the few routine human contacts she has.
  • Sleep. Irregular hours confirmed. She has slept in the seat of her Nomad on a planetary surface rather than return to the mothership.
  • Pauses. She reports stopping partway through sampling to sit or stand on the surface for several minutes with her lamp off, then resuming. She describes these as restful and the examiner accepts that account. If they lengthen, recur without a clear trigger, or occur during hazardous operations, they should be reassessed.
  • Self-auditing. Her perfectionism shows mainly as checking her own work, and she was visibly irritated at finding an engineering item she had marked complete that was not. Under long-term fatigue this kind of self-criticism can intensify.

Recommendations

  1. Cleared for solo long-range operation without restriction on range or duration.
  2. Agree a contact interval before departure. She accepted a check-in with Ray Gateway by message every three weeks of station time while she is in range of a relay, with a "nothing to report" message acceptable. She asked that it not be routed through the station's COVAS. The examiner agreed.
  3. Agree a return condition in her own terms. Asked what would bring her home, she said, "When the hold's full, or when she needs something I can't fix." The examiner has recorded both as her stated criteria and asked her to treat them as binding.
  4. Sleep: keep to a regular rest period aboard Hymn of the Long Night. Sleeping in the Nomad on the surface is not advised as a habit.
  5. Before the next annual examination, disclose the neural interface firmware version and the scope of the COVAS modifications, given the proposed biometric link.
  6. Voluntary counselling offered on return regarding the 3310 Pleiades encounter. She declined. The offer stands.

Examiner's note

Station staff report that out of her flight suit the subject tends to wear little, or noticeably reduced, clothing. Asked about it, she attributed it to the hours spent sealed in a suit and said she "likes to feel the air" once she is out of one. I consider this a reasonable response to prolonged full-body confinement, consistent with what I have seen in other long-duration pilots, and not a concern.
